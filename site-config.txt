# FAQ
# Based on the FAQ currently published by HackerRivals.
# ------------------------------------------------

[FAQ 1]
question=What makes this different from a traditional hackathon?
answer=HackerRivals combines 1-hour online hackathons with 3-hour in-person competitions built for a live audience. In-person events feature audience participation, entertainment and on-stage presentations, and teams qualify for their place before event day.

[FAQ 2]
question=How does the eSports competition format work?
answer=Teams move through three rounds: concept presentation, an elimination round, then a championship final with polished presentations and demos where possible.

[FAQ 3]
question=How can the audience participate in the competition?
answer=The audience votes during the event, helps choose the Whammy challenge and plays a direct role in deciding which teams advance and win.

[FAQ 4]
anchor=qualify
question=Who can participate and how do teams qualify?
answer=Teams can have one to five members. In-person events have a limited number of team spots, decided through online qualification. Two weeks before, all applicant teams complete an online challenge to determine who qualifies. For the largest major in-person events, a 1-hour online hackathon serves as that selection round. Successful teams advance to the 3-hour in-person competition.

[FAQ 5]
question=What are the technology requirements and sponsor integrations?
answer=Cloud credits and sponsor technologies may be provided. Sponsor tools can be part of challenges, while competition development happens live during the event.

[FAQ 6]
question=What entertainment elements can spectators expect?
answer=Expect a produced live show with an MC, big screens, voting, live statistics and interactive audience moments throughout the competition.

[FAQ 7]
anchor=final
question=What are the prizes and recognition?
answer=In the final round of the in-person event, the audience and judges vote on the winner. Cash prizes and the HackerRivals trophy are awarded on stage immediately. Sponsor credits, swag and other benefits can vary by event.

[FAQ 8]
question=Where exactly is the event located?
answer=The currently published FAQ lists Northeastern University at 375 Queen St W in Toronto, with a nearby VIP after party location for that event.

[FAQ 9]
question=How do tickets work and why do you charge?
answer=Ticketing depends on the event. Larger host events manage their own tickets; standalone events may charge to control attendance, with proceeds supporting the nominated charity.

[FAQ 10]
anchor=build
question=What amenities and support are provided?
answer=At the in-person event, teams build in a dedicated room with food and drinks provided. Other support varies by event and may include WiFi, workshops, sponsor demos and technical support.

[FAQ 11]
anchor=challenge
question=How should I prepare for the competition?
answer=Practice the relevant technologies, but do not build the competition project in advance. The creative challenge is shared with qualified teams on the morning of the in-person event; development begins only after it is released.

[FAQ 12]
question=What happens if I want to attend as a spectator only?
answer=Spectators can watch the rounds, participate in voting, enjoy the production, network and take part in the live HackerRivals experience.

# COMPETITION RULES
# Condensed from the current published HackerRivals rules.
# ------------------------------------------------

[RULE 1]
title=Team composition
text=Teams may have up to five members, with one registered leader. Extra human help outside the registered team can cause immediate elimination.

[RULE 2]
title=Onboarding
text=Selected teams must attend mandatory onboarding calls. Missing onboarding can put the team place at risk.

[RULE 3]
title=Project assignment
text=The official assignment is revealed on the morning of the event. Project development cannot begin before it is released.

[RULE 4]
title=Use of AI
text=AI is encouraged for coding, design, ideation and support, but no extra human helpers beyond the registered team are allowed.

[RULE 5]
title=Intellectual property
text=Teams keep ownership of their IP while granting HackerRivals permission to capture and share event media and project descriptions.

[RULE 6]
title=Selection and participation
text=In-person events have a limited number of team spots, determined through online qualification. Two weeks before, all applicant teams take part in an online challenge; for the largest major events, this is a 1-hour online hackathon. Teams that qualify advance to the 3-hour in-person event. Teams are selected by the HackerRivals committee, and there is no competitor registration fee.

[RULE 7]
title=Equipment and internet
text=Teams bring their own laptops, peripherals and adapters. Internet is provided, but speed and stability are not guaranteed.

[RULE 8]
title=Age
text=There is no strict age restriction, although age information may be collected for venue or partner requirements.

[RULE 9]
title=Attendance and stage time
text=Late arrival can lead to disqualification. Teams must follow presentation and Q and A time limits on stage.

[RULE 10]
title=Demos and judging
text=Teams receive fixed demo and Q and A slots. Judging criteria and deadlines are shared during onboarding and on event day.

[RULE 11]
title=Safety and compliance
text=Projects must be legal and safe. Teams must follow venue requirements, technical limits and the HackerRivals Code of Conduct.

[FAQ 13]
question=Are the 1-hour online hackathons always qualifiers?
answer=No. Our 1-hour online hackathons are often standalone events. Some are held two weeks before a major 3-hour in-person event to decide which teams qualify for the in-person competition.

[FAQ 14]
anchor=arrival
question=Where do qualified teams compete?
answer=Qualified teams attend the event venue in person for the 3-hour competition. Arrive ready to build and present on stage; follow the arrival and onboarding instructions for your event.

[FAQ 15]
anchor=first-pitch
question=What happens in the first pitch round?
answer=Each team gets five minutes on stage to pitch its idea to the audience in the first round of the in-person competition.

[FAQ 16]
anchor=whammy
question=What is the Whammy?
answer=After the first pitch round, an audience-voted Whammy challenge is revealed. It adds a curve ball that teams must incorporate into their solution.

[FAQ 17]
anchor=elimination
question=How does the elimination round work?
answer=Each team gets another five minutes to present its idea, architecture and a demo if possible. The audience votes to choose the two finalists.
