Two weeks before: the selection round

All teams applying for an Arena event take part in an online challenge two weeks beforehand to determine who gets through. For our largest major events, this selection round takes the form of a 1-hour online hackathon. Successful teams advance to the 3-hour in-person event.

Special Gathering · App Championship

Build it. Let it compete.

Gathering AGI starts with app creation. The two best apps are published through HackerRivals' Apple and Google store accounts, and the leaderboard is updated. Two or more apps then compete in an App Championship lasting up to 18 months.

  1. Create & qualify

    Build at Gathering AGI. The two best apps are selected for publication and leaderboard recognition.

  2. Publish & improve

    Competing apps stay completely free. Teams can keep improving their apps during the championship, until the final three-month freeze begins.

  3. Three-month freeze

    During the final three months of the championship, teams lose access to the HackerRivals store accounts and no further app changes may be made. Apps must continue functioning through the final.

  4. Championship final

    The winner is announced, extra points are added to the global leaderboard and prize money is awarded and recorded in the leaderboard listing. An app must remain functional through the championship final to qualify for the prize.

  5. Graduate or retire

    Teams have 60 days after the final to transfer their existing store listings to their own accounts. After that window, apps that have not graduated are retired and removed from the HackerRivals stores.

Room for the final

No Gathering AGI events will be held within the four months leading up to an App Championship final. The three-month freeze is part of the championship period, not an extra three months after it.

App publishing, ownership & creator credit

Teams retain their intellectual property. They give HackerRivals the rights needed to publish and promote the competition app during the championship, with credit to the team and the individual creators. Publishing under HackerRivals' accounts gives the organizers access to the store metrics used to score the championship.

Teams decide ownership among themselves. HackerRivals suggests equal ownership, but does not determine the split or whether an individual may publish the app.

Teams may publish their own version during the competition using a different app name, provided it is sufficiently distinct to comply with Apple and Google rules. If a team's version causes a store-policy conflict, the team must correct its own version in HackerRivals' favor so the HackerRivals competition listing does not need to change. Every listing must comply with the relevant store's rules.

After the final, teams can transfer the existing listing to their own Apple or Google developer accounts during the 60-day graduation window, subject to the platform's transfer requirements.

Free apps, PG content & ongoing responsibilities

Competition apps must be completely free on the Apple and Google stores. HackerRivals will not put a price on them. All apps must be PG in content, with no hate speech, violence or other material that could damage HackerRivals' reputation.

Teams pay for hosting and maintain the backend, app and supporting services. HackerRivals covers the app-store account membership fees during the competition. Store publication does not provide backend hosting.

Teams must keep the app functional throughout the championship, including the freeze and final. Deliberate damage or withdrawal of the services needed for the app to function is incompatible with that requirement.

Store branding & notice to app users

During the competition, each app's download page must promote HackerRivals. HackerRivals reserves the right to edit the page to add its branding, and teams must not remove that branding during the competition. This promotion requirement ends after the competition.

The download page must tell users that the app may stop functioning at the end of the competition, which lasts up to 18 months. The graduation window does not guarantee continued service.

Graduation, retirement & the competition archive

At the end of the 60-day graduation window, HackerRivals will retire apps that have not transferred and delete its store listings where the platform permits. Keeping an app running permanently is the team's choice and responsibility.

HackerRivals retains permission to promote and document the app on its website after the competition, including a write-up preserving the competition's history with credit to the team and individuals. Teams are encouraged to send a link to the app's new home so the archive can point users to it.
