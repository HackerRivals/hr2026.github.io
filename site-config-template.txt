# FAQ

[FAQ 1]
category=General
question=What makes this different from a traditional hackathon?
answer=HackerRivals brings the community together through three official formats. Arena is our 3-hour in-person competition built for a live audience. Online offers 1-hour hackathons, often standalone or used as Arena qualifiers. Gatherings include Underground, our secret invite-only networking events, and AGI, our competitions over food and drinks.

[FAQ 2]
category=Arena
question=How does the Arena competition format work?
answer=Teams move through three rounds: concept presentation, an elimination round, then a Gathering AGI competition final with polished presentations and demos where possible.

[FAQ 3]
category=Arena
question=How can the audience participate in the competition?
answer=The audience votes during the event, helps choose the Whammy challenge and plays a direct role in deciding which teams advance and win.

[FAQ 4]
category=Arena
anchor=qualify
question=Who can participate and how do teams qualify?
answer=Teams can have one to five members. Arena has limited team spots, decided through online qualification two weeks before the event. For the largest Arena events, a 1-hour Online hackathon serves as the selection round. Successful teams advance to the 3-hour Arena competition.

[FAQ 5]
category=Arena
question=What are the technology requirements and sponsor integrations?
answer=Cloud credits and sponsor technologies may be provided. Sponsor tools can be part of challenges, while competition development happens live during the event.

[FAQ 6]
category=Arena
question=What entertainment elements can spectators expect?
answer=Expect a produced live show with an MC, big screens, voting, live statistics and interactive audience moments throughout the competition.

[FAQ 7]
category=Arena
anchor=final
question=What are the prizes and recognition?
answer=In the final round of Arena, the audience and judges vote on the winner. Cash prizes and the HackerRivals trophy are awarded on stage immediately. Sponsor credits, swag and other benefits can vary by event. Leaderboard point awards and prizes are set per event.

[FAQ 8]
category=Arena
question=Where exactly is the event located?
answer=The currently published FAQ lists Northeastern University at 375 Queen St W in Toronto, with a nearby VIP after party location for that event.

[FAQ 9]
category=General
question=How do tickets work and why do you charge?
answer=All competitors receive a free ticket to the event so they can compete. Competitors who use that ticket to enter but then deliberately skip the competition will be removed from the event immediately and banned from all future HackerRivals events, including as a paid attendee or an online contestant. This policy protects the time and places reserved for genuine competitors. Spectator ticketing varies by event.

[FAQ 10]
category=Arena
anchor=build
question=What amenities and support are provided?
answer=At the in-person event, teams build in a dedicated room with food and drinks provided. Other support varies by event and may include WiFi, workshops, sponsor demos and technical support.

[FAQ 11]
category=Arena
anchor=challenge
question=How should I prepare for the competition?
answer=Practice the relevant technologies, but do not build the competition project in advance. The creative challenge is shared with qualified teams on the morning of the in-person event; development begins only after it is released.

[FAQ 12]
category=Arena
question=What happens if I want to attend as a spectator only?
answer=Spectators can watch the rounds, participate in voting, enjoy the production, network and take part in the live HackerRivals experience.

# Gathering AGI app competition policies

[FAQ 18]
category=Gatherings
anchor=app-championship
question=How does the 12-month Gathering AGI app competition work?
answer=At each Gathering AGI, teams build and share apps, then everyone tries them and votes to select the top two. These apps enter the 12-month Gathering AGI app competition and are made available free through HackerRivals' Apple App Store and Google Play accounts, subject to store review. Teams can improve their apps for the first nine months; the final three months are a freeze. At the end, the winning app earns additional leaderboard points and prize money set for that event. Teams then have 60 days to transfer their listings to their own developer accounts or have the apps retired.

[FAQ 19]
category=Gatherings
anchor=app-freeze
question=What changes during the three-month freeze?
answer=The final three months of the 12-month Gathering AGI app competition are a freeze, not three additional months. Teams lose access to the HackerRivals store accounts and cannot make further app changes. They remain responsible for keeping the app and backend functional through the final to qualify for prizes. No Gathering AGI event takes place within the four months preceding the final.

[FAQ 20]
category=Gatherings
anchor=app-costs
question=Who pays to keep Gathering AGI apps running?
answer=Teams pay hosting costs and maintain the app, backend and supporting services. HackerRivals covers the app-store account membership fees during the competition. Every competition app is completely free on Apple and Google stores and must remain functional through the final to qualify for prize money.

[FAQ 21]
category=Gatherings
anchor=app-ownership
question=Who owns the app, and can teams publish their own version?
answer=Teams retain their IP and decide ownership among themselves, including whether individuals may publish the app; equal ownership is suggested, not imposed. HackerRivals receives the rights needed to publish and promote the competition app during the Gathering AGI competition, with credit to the team and individual creators, plus continuing permission for a credited website archive. Teams may publish their own version during the competition under a different name, provided it is sufficiently distinct and complies with Apple and Google rules. If their version causes a store-policy conflict, the team must correct its own version in HackerRivals' favor so the HackerRivals competition listing does not need to change.

[FAQ 22]
category=Gatherings
anchor=app-graduation
question=What happens to apps after the 12-month Gathering AGI competition?
answer=Teams have 60 days after the Gathering AGI competition final to transfer their existing store listings to their own Apple or Google developer accounts, subject to the platform's transfer requirements. After that window, HackerRivals retires apps that have not graduated, removes them from its stores and deletes the listings where permitted. HackerRivals retains permission to promote and document the app on its website, including a write-up preserving competition history with credit to the team and individual creators. Teams are encouraged to share a link to the app's new home for the archive. Continued app service is not guaranteed.

[FAQ 23]
category=Gatherings
anchor=app-store
question=What content and branding rules apply to competition apps?
answer=Apps must be PG, with no hate speech, violence or material that could damage HackerRivals' reputation. Their download pages must promote HackerRivals during the competition; HackerRivals may edit those pages to add branding, which teams must not remove during the competition. Pages must warn that the app may stop functioning at the end of the competition, lasting 12 months. The branding requirement ends after the competition.

Teams can improve their apps until the final three-month freeze, when team store access is removed and no more changes may be made. No Gatherings: AGI events occur within the four months before a Gathering AGI competition final.

Pages must warn users that the app may stop functioning at the end of the competition, lasting 12 months. The branding requirement ends after the competition.

[FAQ 24]
category=Gatherings
anchor=gatherings
question=What are Gatherings: Underground and Gatherings: AGI?
answer=Gatherings: Underground are secret, invite-only networking events. Gathering AGI (Advanced Gathering Intelligence) is a social app-building competition over food and drinks. Everyone joins a team of at least two people, builds an app, tests the results and votes for the top two apps to enter the 12-month Gathering AGI app competition. Arena remains HackerRivals' flagship 3-hour in-person competition; Online remains the 1-hour online hackathon, sometimes used as an Arena qualifier.

[FAQ 25]
category=Gatherings
anchor=agi-judging
question=How are Gathering AGI results, leaderboard points and prizes decided?
answer=Everyone at the Gathering AGI event tests the apps and votes for the top two teams, whose apps enter the 12-month Gathering AGI app competition. The longer competition uses metrics accessible through HackerRivals' Apple and Google store accounts to score participating apps. The eventual winner receives additional leaderboard points and cash prizes, with amounts set for the event. Apps must remain functional through the final to qualify. Arena and Online have their own separate judging arrangements.

[FAQ 26]
category=Gatherings
anchor=agi-agenda
question=What happens at a Gathering AGI event, step by step?
answer=First, allow 30 minutes for everyone to find or form teams of at least two people; the event does not move on until everyone is on a team. Next comes 15 minutes of team ideation, 15 minutes for teams to share their ideas with the room, and 30 minutes to build an app. After building, everyone gets to try out the apps, supported by HackerRivals' Apple and Google app-store publishing arrangements, subject to review and availability. Everyone then votes for the top two teams, whose apps progress into the 12-month Gathering AGI app competition. The first four timed stages take 90 minutes; testing and voting follow.
