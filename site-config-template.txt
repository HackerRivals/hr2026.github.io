# FAQ

[FAQ 1]
question=What makes this different from a traditional hackathon?
answer=HackerRivals brings the community together through Arena, Online and Gatherings. Arena is the 3-hour live audience competition; Online offers 1-hour challenges, often standalone or used as Arena qualifiers. Special Gathering AGI events select apps for an App Championship lasting up to 18 months.

[FAQ 2]
question=How does the Arena competition format work?
answer=Teams move through three rounds: concept presentation, an elimination round, then a championship final with polished presentations and demos where possible.

[FAQ 3]
question=How can the audience participate in the competition?
answer=The audience votes during the event, helps choose the Whammy challenge and plays a direct role in deciding which teams advance and win.

[FAQ 4]
anchor=qualify
question=Who can participate and how do teams qualify?
answer=Teams can have one to five members. Arena has limited team spots, decided through online qualification two weeks before the event. For the largest Arena events, a 1-hour Online hackathon serves as the selection round. Successful teams advance to the 3-hour Arena competition.

[FAQ 5]
question=What are the technology requirements and sponsor integrations?
answer=Cloud credits and sponsor technologies may be provided. Sponsor tools can be part of challenges, while competition development happens live during the event.

[FAQ 6]
question=What entertainment elements can spectators expect?
answer=Expect a produced live show with an MC, big screens, voting, live statistics and interactive audience moments throughout the competition.

[FAQ 7]
anchor=final
question=What are the prizes and recognition?
answer=At the Arena final, the audience and judges vote on the winner. Cash prizes and the HackerRivals trophy are awarded on stage immediately. Separately, an App Championship final announces its winner and awards extra leaderboard points and prize money, which is recorded in the leaderboard listing. Sponsor credits, swag and other benefits vary by event.

[FAQ 8]
question=Where exactly is the event located?
answer=The currently published FAQ lists Northeastern University at 375 Queen St W in Toronto, with a nearby VIP after party location for that event.

[FAQ 9]
question=How do tickets work and why do you charge?
answer=All competitors receive a free ticket to the event so they can compete. Competitors who use that ticket to enter but then deliberately skip the competition will be removed from the event immediately and banned from all future HackerRivals events, including as a paid attendee or an online contestant. This policy protects the time and places reserved for genuine competitors. Spectator ticketing varies by event.

[FAQ 10]
anchor=build
question=What amenities and support are provided?
answer=At the in-person event, teams build in a dedicated room with food and drinks provided. Other support varies by event and may include WiFi, workshops, sponsor demos and technical support.

[FAQ 11]
anchor=challenge
question=How should I prepare for the competition?
answer=Practice the relevant technologies, but do not build the competition project in advance. The creative challenge is shared with qualified teams on the morning of the in-person event; development begins only after it is released.

[FAQ 12]
question=What happens if I want to attend as a spectator only?
answer=Spectators can watch the rounds, participate in voting, enjoy the production, network and take part in the live HackerRivals experience.

# COMPETITION RULES

[RULE 1]
title=Team composition
text=Teams may have up to five members, with one registered leader. Extra human help outside the registered team can cause immediate elimination.

[RULE 2]
title=Onboarding
text=Selected teams must attend mandatory onboarding calls. Missing onboarding can put the team place at risk.

[RULE 3]
title=Arena project assignment
text=The official assignment is revealed on the morning of the event. Project development cannot begin before it is released.

[RULE 4]
title=Use of AI
text=AI is encouraged for coding, design, ideation and support, but no extra human helpers beyond the registered team are allowed.

[RULE 5]
title=Intellectual property
text=Teams retain ownership of their IP and grant HackerRivals permission to share event media and project descriptions. Gathering AGI championship apps also grant competition publishing and promotional rights, with credit to the team and individual creators, plus continuing permission for credited website promotion and an archive after the competition. Teams decide their own ownership split; equal ownership is suggested, not required.

[RULE 6]
title=Selection and participation
text=Arena has limited team spots, determined through online qualification two weeks before the event; for the largest Arena events, this is a 1-hour Online hackathon. Qualifying teams advance to the 3-hour Arena competition. Teams are selected by the HackerRivals committee, and there is no competitor registration fee.

[RULE 7]
title=Equipment and internet
text=Teams bring their own laptops, peripherals and adapters. Internet is provided, but speed and stability are not guaranteed.

[RULE 8]
title=Age
text=There is no strict age restriction, although age information may be collected for venue or partner requirements.

[RULE 9]
title=Attendance and stage time
text=Late arrival can lead to disqualification. Teams must follow presentation and Q and A time limits on stage.

[RULE 10]
title=Demos and judging
text=Arena teams receive fixed demo and Q and A slots. Judging criteria and deadlines are shared during onboarding and on event day. Gathering AGI selects the two best apps for publication; championship scoring uses metrics accessible through HackerRivals' store accounts. Results update the leaderboard, with extra points and prize money recorded for the App Championship winner.

[RULE 11]
title=Safety and compliance
text=Projects must be legal and safe. Teams must follow venue requirements, technical limits and the HackerRivals Code of Conduct.

# Gathering AGI and App Championship policies

[FAQ 18]
anchor=app-championship
question=How does the Gathering AGI App Championship work?
answer=The two best apps from Gathering AGI are published under HackerRivals' store accounts and the leaderboard is updated. Two or more apps compete for up to 18 months, with improvements allowed until the final three-month freeze. At the final, the winner receives extra leaderboard points and prize money, recorded in the leaderboard listing.

[FAQ 19]
anchor=app-freeze
question=What changes during the three-month freeze?
answer=The final three months of the championship are a freeze: teams lose access to the HackerRivals store accounts and no more app changes may be made. Teams remain responsible for keeping the app and backend functional through the championship final to qualify for the prize. No Gathering AGI events take place in the four months leading up to a final.

[FAQ 20]
anchor=app-costs
question=Who pays to keep championship apps running?
answer=Teams pay hosting costs and maintain the app, backend and supporting services. HackerRivals covers the app-store account membership fees during the competition. Every competition app is completely free on Apple and Google stores and must remain functional through the final to qualify for prize money.

[FAQ 21]
anchor=app-ownership
question=Who owns the app, and can teams publish their own version?
answer=Teams retain their IP and decide ownership among themselves; equal ownership is suggested, not imposed. HackerRivals receives publishing and promotional rights for the championship and continuing permission for a credited website archive. Teams may publish their own version during the competition with a different name, provided it is sufficiently distinct and complies with Apple and Google rules. Any conflict caused by the team's version must be corrected in HackerRivals' favor.

[FAQ 22]
anchor=app-graduation
question=What happens to apps after the championship final?
answer=Teams have a 60-day graduation window to transfer their existing store listings to their own developer accounts, subject to Apple and Google transfer requirements. After that, apps that have not graduated are retired and removed from the HackerRivals stores. HackerRivals preserves credited write-ups and may link to a team's new app listing. Continued app service is not guaranteed.

[FAQ 23]
anchor=app-store
question=What content and branding rules apply to competition apps?
answer=Apps must be PG, with no hate speech, violence or material that could damage HackerRivals' reputation. Their download pages must promote HackerRivals during the competition; HackerRivals may edit those pages to add branding, which teams must not remove during the competition. Pages must warn that the app may stop functioning at the end of the competition, lasting up to 18 months. The branding requirement ends after the competition.

[RULE 12]
title=App Championship lifecycle
text=The two best Gathering AGI apps are published through HackerRivals' Apple and Google store accounts and the leaderboard is updated. Two or more apps compete for up to 18 months. Teams can improve their apps until the final three-month freeze, when team store access is removed and no more changes may be made. No Gathering AGI events occur within the four months before a championship final.

[RULE 13]
title=Free apps, content and store branding
text=Competition apps must be completely free and PG, with no hate speech, violence or content harmful to HackerRivals' reputation. Download pages must promote HackerRivals during the competition. HackerRivals may edit the pages to add branding, which teams must not remove during the competition. Pages must warn users that the app may stop functioning at the end of the competition, lasting up to 18 months. The branding requirement ends after the competition.

[RULE 14]
title=Hosting, maintenance and prize eligibility
text=Teams are responsible for hosting costs and maintenance of the app, backend and supporting services. HackerRivals covers app-store account membership fees during the competition. An app must remain functional through the championship final to qualify for the prize, including during the freeze.

[RULE 15]
title=Independent team publications
text=Teams may publish their own version during the competition using a different name, provided it is sufficiently distinct and complies with Apple and Google rules. If their version causes a store-policy conflict, teams must correct their version in HackerRivals' favor so HackerRivals does not have to change its competition listing.

[RULE 16]
title=Graduation, retirement and creator archive
text=After the final, teams have 60 days to transfer existing listings to their own developer accounts, subject to store requirements. HackerRivals then retires apps that have not graduated, removes them from its stores and deletes the listings where permitted. HackerRivals retains permission for credited website write-ups and promotion preserving the competition history. Teams are encouraged to provide links to their app's new home.
